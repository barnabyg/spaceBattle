# Hard-physics rules

Every mechanic must be plausible under known physics. The technology can be far ahead of ours, but it never breaks a physical law. This rules out several sci-fi conventions a reader might expect:

- **No faster-than-light travel.** Lasers travel at light speed; everything else is slower.
- **Full Newtonian movement in three dimensions.** Ships have no top speed and feel no drag. The drive is fixed along the ship's long axis, so a ship must rotate with its thrusters before it can push in a new direction, and larger ships turn more slowly.
- **Drives use propellant as well as power.** Each ship has limited delta-v, and a ship running low on propellant must withdraw.
- **Waste heat is a limit.** Reactors, drives and weapons produce heat that only radiators can shed.
- **No energy-absorbing force fields.** Shields are powered defences based on real effects, such as a magnetic field that bends charged-particle beams, or coolant channels that carry laser heat into the ship's heat budget. Each works only against particular kinds of attack.

This is the game's central identity, chosen over the more familiar shields-and-hit-points model. Proposals that loosen these rules should be rejected unless this ADR is revisited first.

## Rules of engagement are not physics

Some rules limit what captains may do rather than what physics allows. The main one: an enemy may keep firing at a ship that is withdrawing, but may not chase it. Rules like this are deliberate game design, not exceptions to the physics, and they belong in the rules of engagement, never in the physics model.
